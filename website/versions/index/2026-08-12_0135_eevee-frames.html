01 |purpose

lith sits on your desk, doing one thing well out the box - helping your focus with a pomodoro timer.

02 |what's inside

Every lith is built from the same small, well-understood parts. That's the whole point, small enough to sit on a desk, open enough to reconfigure.

ESP32-S3-Zero

The brain. WiFi + Bluetooth, USB-C with native CDC, plenty of headroom for custom firmware.

1.9″ TFT LCD display

ST7789V2 panel, 320×170, driven over SPI. Sharp enough for a timer, flexible enough for whatever you make next.

Rotary encoder

The orange scroll wheel. Turns into a dial, a scrubber, a volume knob, whatever the firmware wants it to be.

Keyboard switches

Two mechanical keys, tap or hold, for start, stop, select, or whatever you assign them.

Coin vibration motor

Haptic feedback driven through an NPN transistor for clean PWM control, a quiet buzz instead of a beep.

03 |knapp it into something else

knap /nap/ · verb · to shape a stone by striking flakes from it; here, to shape your lith into a new tool.

The timer is just the first shape. lith is reconfigurable: chat with Oldowan, our firmware co-design agent, answer a few multiple-choice questions about what you want your lith to do, and it writes custom firmware you flash straight from the browser.

Or write your own, lith is based on the ESP32 s3 and is compatible with Arduino and PlatformIO IDEs

note: knapping needs a laptop or desktop running Chrome or Edge. Flashing happens over USB via Web Serial, which phone browsers, Firefox and Safari don't support.
